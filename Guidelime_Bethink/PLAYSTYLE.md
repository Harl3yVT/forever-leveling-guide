# Play-style preferences

Edit these preferences to match how you want to play.

## Questing and travel

- Compare collection quests by drop reliability, target density, competition, respawns and travel. Prefer predictable completion when rewards are comparable.
- Assume players know ordinary class and combat basics. Give useful general advice once per route; repeat it only when a specific encounter gives it additional value.

## Difficulty and groups

- For solo encounters, +1 is acceptable, generally avoid +2 and exclude +3. Check actual enemies, packs, caves and retreat space; place level gates before danger.
- Assume party members are at the player's current level. For outdoor content, recommend party size and composition in guide text only if there is a specific reason. Avoid plans relying on intentional death or exceptional execution.
- Prefer group encounters around +3; allow higher enemies when party size, composition and encounter mechanics support them. Normally schedule dungeon entry about three levels below the final boss, accounting for actual packs, mechanics and escorts.

## Dungeons

- Plan one clear of each relevant dungeon, honoring configuration exclusions. Favor the dungeon in close comparisons, including a modest XP/hour disadvantage, for equipment rewards and a more varied leveling experience.
- Useful quests and reasonable boat/hearth travel can justify a separate visit. Assume effectively immediate group finding under the realmless model; still budget travel, recovery and clearing.
- Exclude hostile-capital dungeon expeditions. Do not schedule repeats for missing drops or XP.

## Training and professions

- Schedule useful early class training before encounters that need it; use local trainers at level 6 and plan early city visits around level 8 where the starting route supports them. Preserve later city timing where it fits the zone better.
- Skip equipment-only detours without a clear long-term benefit, such as a lasting endgame upgrade. Prioritize earlier useful bag rewards over minor travel savings.
- Keep primary profession choices neutral. Give one action-led overview at the relevant city stop or departure decision: identify local and city training, useful alternatives and scheduled later stops for remaining gaps. Include chosen gathering professions still unlearned after the starter quest book or trainer. Make service-only visits skippable when needs are covered; combine ordinary city visits with quest business. Include Artisan training for progression beyond skill 225 at suitable route stops, including dungeon visits. Make these steps skippable; do not require a skill grind.
- Include secondary professions (First Aid, Cooking and Fishing), each with one tracked initial learning step along planned outings. Keep these lessons required and independent of optional city visits; omit repeated secondary-profession reminders from primary-profession guidance. Include later rank training, books and advancement quests once at suitable route stops. Buy books from original vendors on planned visits; use the Auction House only when no vendor stop fits the route. Make these steps skippable; do not require a skill grind.
- Include skippable weapon-master training for all classes at existing city stops, including Polearms from level 20 where usable. Train missing types for planned weapons; reserve money for class training.
